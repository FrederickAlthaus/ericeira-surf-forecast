How "Great / Good / OK / Bad / No-go" is worked out

Every hour gets a score out of 100, starting at 100 and losing points for things that make it harder to ride: waves bigger (or more powerful) than you and your board want, wind that's too strong or blowing onshore, waves too small to actually catch on your board, and a choppy sea where local wind-waves are swamping the underlying swell. The score then maps to a tier:

ScoreTier
85–100Great
65–84Good
40–64OK
15–39Bad
0–14No-go

Why one beach might read smaller than the raw forecast

The free wave model behind this app reports open-water conditions for each beach's GPS point — it has no way to know about small local features like a harbour breakwater. Praia do Sul is sheltered by Ericeira's harbour wall and reliably runs smaller than the open-water number suggests, so its forecast is deliberately scaled down (currently to 65% of the raw model wave height) based on what's actually been seen there in person — not guessed. This is a starting estimate, not a precise measurement, and worth refining further as more real visits confirm it.

Factors NOT in this app (yet)

A few real factors aren't included because there's no reliable free data source, or because getting it wrong would be worse than leaving it out: which tide each specific beach actually prefers (a beach break can behave very differently at low vs. high tide, but this needs local knowledge per spot), swell direction matching a beach's exact orientation (matters a lot for a point break like Ribeira d'Ilhas), crowd levels, underwater hazards/rip currents, and water temperature (for wetsuit choice). Worth keeping in mind alongside whatever this page says.

These are presets, not a live settings panel — on purpose, so the numbers stay simple and visible in the tables above. If they don't match how you actually surf, open index.html in a text editor and edit the SKILL_LEVELS / BOARD_TYPES objects near the top of the <script> — it's plain numbers, nothing else needs to change.
